# Process overview

## What I built

The course website for SLOP3446: Round Trip - Physical Artefacts Through the Machine and Back, a studio art course that introduces technology into art objects and has students move work back and forth between digital and traditional artistic methods.

## How I got here

It started with wanting a course I'd actually want to take, one no real university would run because of the logistics. The idea inverts Creative Computing: lean less on a purely digital result and more on physical art objects and traditional methods, with digital media or methods still woven into the process somewhere.

Planning what the content would actually be, and how best to present it, used plan mode to settle topics and refine the weekly outline. In that stage I added a clause to CLAUDE.md requiring the course to work for two distinct audiences, programmers and artists, with materials supporting both. That forced a drastic reordering of topics, giving each group a better first few weeks leading into the first assignment. [`2de6210...17f9507`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-QuackyDuck826/compare/2de6210...17f9507)

Producing the content itself needed more finesse. I gave one fully worked example week, a short lecture broken down plus steps for an activity and had Claude extrapolate the rest from the topic guide. The results were reasonable ideas but not the depth a real lecture or tutorial slot needs, and pushing Claude to expand further on its own, it clearly struggled without more help. My fix was a better reference: since the ANU School of Art doesn't put much online, I gave it EXTN1019's lab collection instead (Creative Computing's year 11/12 counterpart). Working from that example, it roughly tripled the depth of each tutorial's notes into much deeper processes and ideas. [`fa9ad27...8ec7d07`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-QuackyDuck826/compare/fa9ad27...8ec7d07)

With the tutorial tasks designed, I planned each week's short lecture as a quick first-pass demo of what students do that week, plus ideas that extend the taught techniques further than the course explicitly requires. Every lecture page carries a slide deck meant to run alongside a lecturer demoing the actual process in code, in another program, or on a physical object. [`6fd7668...f68b44a`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-QuackyDuck826/compare/6fd7668...f68b44a)

A gallery page collects every extended-technique example from the end of each lecture together in one place. [`c4504cd`](https://github.com/comp4020-agentic-coding-studio/comp4020-ass2-QuackyDuck826/commit/c4504cd)

Visual polish came last, staying inside the brand palette. On top of the designated gold, I added a complementary blue, used in the banner patterns on every page. Small profile pictures came from DiceBear, recoloured to map directly onto the brand palette. The home page hero image was trickier: once shifted to brand-compliant colours, it lost too much resolution to fill a full-width banner cleanly, and even a cleaner, slightly off-colour version still looked crusty blown up to banner size. I leaned into that instead of fighting it, adding a soft blur so the image reads as intentionally clouded rather than low-resolution, and sits better behind the text.
