# Projection Mapping

Throwing computed light back onto the physical surface you scanned

---

## Recap

- last week you captured a surface as data — this week you send computed
  light back onto that same kind of surface
- mapping a projector onto an irregular physical object, not a flat screen
- calibration: why a straight rectangle projected onto a curved object goes
  wrong, and how to fix it
- Studio Brief 1 opens today, asking you to combine this pillar with next
  week's programming on-ramp

---

## Setting up, step by step

1. Aim a projector at a stand or table where your object can sit still for
   the rest of the session.
2. Settle the object on that stand before you calibrate, not after — a
   calibration only holds for one fixed relationship between projector and
   object.
3. Treat both the object and the projector as fixed from here — a bump to
   either means calibrating again from scratch.

---

## Calibrating, step by step

1. **Corners first** — match the projected image's corners to the object's
   outer edges. This gets position and scale roughly right.
2. **Curves and angles second** — adjust for what a flat grid can't
   predict: a curved face smears or doubles a straight-on image, an angled
   face stretches it.
3. Nudge the projection, or reshape the source image, until it sits on the
   geometry you actually have rather than floating over it.
4. Map computed imagery onto the calibrated surface — it now reads as part
   of the object, not a rectangle of light sitting on top of it.
5. Capture a still image or clip before you strike the setup — a live
   projection only exists in the room while it's running.
