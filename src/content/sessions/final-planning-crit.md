## Before the session

Bring a one-page plan: the theme, the three-plus techniques, and the biggest
risk as you see it.

## In the session

1. **Deliver a short pitch of your one-page plan**: the theme in one
   sentence, the three-plus techniques, and the biggest risk. A good pitch
   states the theme as a single committed sentence rather than a range of
   options still being weighed — "an installation where a scanned object's
   shape drives a rule-based light pattern" survives scrutiny in a way
   "something with scanning and maybe some lights or sound" doesn't.
2. **Take feedback focused on scope and risk rather than execution** —
   nothing is built yet, so feedback on colour choices or code style is
   premature; what's useful at this stage is someone else testing whether
   your three-plus techniques actually combine or just sit next to each
   other. A plan that lists scanning, a rule system, and fabrication
   independently, with no stated point where one drives another, has the
   same problem Studio Brief 1's brief warns against: a scan sitting next to
   a sketch isn't a combination.
3. **Check the feedback against your plan for hours it quietly assumes but
   the remaining weeks don't have**, before you've spent any of them. This
   is the risk that sinks capstones most often: a plan that's conceptually
   sound but assumes more studio time than the weeks between now and the
   final crit actually provide. Do this check now, while changing the plan
   costs you a rewrite of one page rather than a rebuilt project in week 11.

## Afterwards

The plan itself is marked holistically — see the [assessment
page](/assessments/final-planning/) — and it's what the final crit in week 12
compares your finished project against.
