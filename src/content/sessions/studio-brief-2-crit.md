## Before the session

Bring Studio Brief 2, running, and the one decision you're least sure about.
Same shape as Studio Brief 1's crit, with more techniques on the table.

## In the session

1. **Demo Studio Brief 2 live, in under two minutes** — same shape as Studio
   Brief 1's crit, with more techniques on the table. The brief asks you to
   close the loop — signal from the physical world, through a
   microcontroller, into a program, back out to a physical effect — so the
   demo needs to show that whole loop live. Showing only the sensor feeding
   into a sketch, without the effect coming back out the other side, is the
   SB1-era habit to drop here.
2. **Name which techniques from weeks 1–6 it combines and the decision
   you're least sure about.** By week 7 you've accumulated far more
   techniques than you had for Studio Brief 1, so the harder discipline is
   naming the two or three that are actually load-bearing rather than
   listing everything you touched — the brief marks how the techniques
   connect, not how many appear.
3. **Take feedback aimed at that named decision.** Because the bridging
   week's serial comms is usually what makes or breaks this brief, the
   decision you're least sure about is often exactly there — a baud rate, a
   framing choice, a debounce — and that's the feedback worth prioritising
   over anything about the sketch's visuals.
4. **Mention if your studio pair helped you across a gap in your own
   background** — it's useful context for the feedback, not a confession.
   Week 7 is precisely the week the pairing structure set up in week 1 has
   the art-proficient half leaning on their programming-proficient pair for
   the state-machine material, so saying so is expected, not an admission of
   a shortcut.
