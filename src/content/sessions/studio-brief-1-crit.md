## Before the session

Bring Studio Brief 1, running, and the one decision you're least sure about.

## In the session

1. **Demo Studio Brief 1 live rather than as slides**, in under two minutes,
   in studio-pair order. The piece running is the demo — this is the first
   time your studio pair's combined work meets an audience, and the
   two-minute cap exists to force you to show the digitizing technique
   actually driving (or being driven by) the Programming I idea, rather
   than describe what it would do if it worked. Presenting in pair order
   lets Idris see both halves of a pairing's contribution back to back,
   which matters given the brief asks each of you to name what you
   individually contributed.
2. **Name which digitizing technique and which Programming I idea it
   combines, and the decision you're least sure about.** The useful version
   of "the decision you're least sure about" is one specific choice — "I
   don't know if the scan's mesh is clean enough to drive the sketch
   reliably" — not a general request to be told whether the piece is good.
   Naming everything you're unsure of, or nothing, both waste the two
   minutes the room has for you.
3. **Take feedback aimed at that named decision rather than at polish.**
   With a full studio-pair list to get through in one session, feedback on
   the specific decision you flagged is the only kind that's actionable in
   the time left; general polish notes don't tell you what to change before
   your slot in the show, or before the time built into this session's end.
4. **Use the time built into the end of the session to act on the feedback
   before the day ends.** This crit is formative as much as evaluative —
   tutors want to see the feedback actually applied, not banked for a
   "later" that the brief's two-minute-video deadline doesn't leave room
   for. Walking away without touching the piece again is the most common
   way this step gets skipped.

## Afterwards
