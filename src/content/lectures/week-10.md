The other half of complex physical media, and the piece that closes the
course's thesis: a digital model or a computed decision returned to physical
form through 3D printing or laser cutting. Where week 1 asked "how do you get
matter into the machine," this week asks the reverse question.

## Outline

- 3D printing and laser cutting: what each is good at, and where each fails
- designing for fabrication constraints (tolerances, material behaviour)
  rather than designing on screen and hoping
- a fabricated part as the physical end of a pipeline that started with
  tracking, a rule system, or a sensor
- studio week next: two parallel clinic tracks to shore up whichever half of
  the toolkit you're less confident in, before the final
