## Late work and extensions

Studio Briefs 1 and 2, Final Planning & Ideation, and the Final Project are
all due at noon on their listed date. Work submitted late loses 5% of the
available mark per day, up to five days, after which it is marked as not
submitted — because a crit is a fixed event with an audience in the room, and
a project that isn't ready to show on the day hasn't met the brief yet
regardless of how the mark is calculated.

Extensions are available for illness or misadventure, requested before the
deadline where possible. Ask the teacher running that week's crit, not the
convenor directly, unless the request concerns the convenor's own session.
An extension moves your submission deadline; it doesn't move the crit itself
— if you can't attend the session, arrange to present in the following week's
studio time instead.

## AI-assisted studio work

Agentic coding tools are the subject of this course, not a special case
within it, so there's no separate disclosure form: name what you used and
what for in the same place you'd name any other assistance — a `README`,
commit message, or the "what you submit" section of a brief names it as
`declared`. What's marked is the response to the brief and the judgement
behind it, not whether a human or a tool typed the code, wired the circuit,
or ran the fabrication job.

The one line that isn't negotiable: work presented as running at a crit has
to actually run, live, in the room. A recording of a tool having made
something work once, shown in place of the thing itself working now, doesn't
meet any brief in this course.

## Lab and hardware safety

Weeks 4–6 and 10 put you in the electronics bench and the fabrication lab.

- Electronics (Arduino weeks): never power a board from mains directly; use
  the bench supplies or USB. Check polarity before connecting a battery.
  Report any component that gets noticeably hot to a tutor and stop using it.
- Fabrication (week 10): laser cutters and 3D printers run supervised only —
  a tutor starts the job after checking your file. Safety glasses on
  whenever the laser is running, no exceptions, including "just checking
  something."
- Either lab: no eating or drinking at the bench. If you're not sure a
  material is safe to cut, print, or solder, ask before you run the job, not
  after.

Anyone not following the above is asked to stop for the session; a second
instance is a conversation with the convenor, not the tutor on duty.
